This month I joined a research group led by a newly appointed distinguished professor. Compared with the other groups at our university, the resources seem fairly good. I previously emailed two professors here who work on embodied intelligence, but neither replied. Maybe talking to them in person would have worked better. Maybe it would not have mattered. Both emails were read and ignored.

There are probably gains and losses in every choice. The new professor needs students, so he seems to pay more attention to the students working under him, including me. In practice, I mostly coordinate with the senior student who supervises me.

The professor asked whether I wanted to lead a project myself or work on one with the senior student. Since this is my first research project and I have almost no experience, I decided to follow along and learn the process first.

Research feels like a process with very sparse rewards. The reward only arrives when you produce a milestone result. Most of the time, it is frustration. I still do not know how to get used to this process.

When reading papers, I used to focus on the architecture designed by the authors. I did not pay enough attention to things like experimental design. I usually skim papers, of course, but sometimes the experiments are worth reading carefully because they reveal the authors' actual ideas.

One night recently, I was going to see my girlfriend the next day, so I did not plan to do anything that evening. Once I had free time, though, I realized that apart from playing basketball, I did not seem to have anything I wanted to do. I had no book I particularly wanted to read, no anime I was eager to watch, and no interest in games. I ended up finishing the fourth season of *Jujutsu Kaisen*.

Maybe I should build a richer set of hobbies (?).
